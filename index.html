The Girl Behind The Byline

Before the code, there was curiosity.

I come from a country that is rarely the first name people associate with technology. That has never been a reason for me to stay outside the room.

My story started with a simple question: why? Why does a system work? Why does an application fail? Why can't an idea become something real?

At ESIH, those questions became algorithms, databases, software engineering and long nights in front of a screen. I learned that technology is not magic. It is a language — and languages can be learned.

Today I move between software development, data, IT support and the cloud. I am still learning. But I am no longer simply looking for a place in technology.

I am learning how to build one.

“My background is not the limitation of my story. It is part of what makes the story worth telling.”

The Gate

Wrong passport.
Wrong stereotype.
Right ambition.

“There are fields where people like me are still expected to be the exception.”

The story is bigger than a résumé.

Being a Haitian woman entering technology means carrying two truths at once: the industry is changing, and there is still work to do.

I don't want my identity to be a decorative sentence at the top of a CV. I want it to become evidence that talent can come from unexpected places.

So I keep learning. I build software. I work with data. I troubleshoot systems. I ask questions. I show up.

And every project becomes another small argument against the idea that technology belongs to someone else.

Proof is better than potential.

What I am building.

I am not trying to collect technology names. I am building the ability to move from an idea to a working solution — from database to interface, from data to insight, from problem to system.

The stack changes. The principle doesn't: understand the problem, build carefully, learn from what breaks, and make it better.
